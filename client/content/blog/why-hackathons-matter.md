Hackathons get a reputation for being intense, competitive, and only for people who already know how to code. That picture leaves a lot of people out - and it's not the experience we want at [DUWiT Hacks](https://duwithacks.com).

## Learning by doing

A 24-hour hackathon compresses months of curiosity into one weekend. You pick a problem, form a team, and ship something. That loop teaches more than any lecture: how to scope work, ask for help, and recover when things break at 2 a.m.

## Community over competition

Women-in-tech events aren't about winning a single prize. They're about meeting people who share your interests, finding mentors, and realising you're not the only one in the room who wondered whether tech was "for them."

At DUWiT Hacks, we design for beginners and returners as much as for experienced builders. Questions are expected. Impostor syndrome is common. Showing up is the hard part - and it's enough.

## A launchpad, not a finish line

Many attendees leave with their first portfolio project, a LinkedIn connection, or a clearer sense of what they want to study next. Some come back as organisers or mentors. The hackathon is a starting point.

If you're thinking about joining us in 2027, you don't need a perfect idea or a finished résumé. Bring curiosity, a laptop, and willingness to try. We'll handle the rest.
